Amazon Leo announced on November 13, 2026 that its first Vulcan launch (LV‑02) will carry 40 low‑Earth‑orbit satellites. The launch follows a broader plan to deploy more than 1,500 first‑generation satellites across 38 Vulcan flights, the fastest cadence in the program. Reaching this milestone pushes the constellation closer to the FCC‑authorized 3,236 satellites needed for global broadband service. The mission also showcases Amazon’s new Cape Canaveral infrastructure, where hourly workers have been trained as satellite technicians.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Vulcan launch accelerates Amazon Leo’s path to a full‑scale internet constellation.</Callout>

## What happened
Amazon Leo’s LV‑02 Vulcan mission will loft 40 production satellites, the first batch launched on the new heavy‑lift vehicle. The launch comes just weeks after LV‑01, made possible by Amazon’s vertical‑integration facility and mobile launch platform at Cape Canaveral.

The company’s launch cadence is set to double its annual rate, targeting over 1,500 first‑generation satellites across 38 Vulcan missions. This aggressive schedule is part of a broader strategy that already saw 396 satellites placed in orbit by July 2026.

Regulatory pressure has eased after the FCC waived the original July 30 2026 deadline for deploying half of the 3,236‑satellite constellation, giving Amazon more flexibility to meet its rollout goals.

## Why it matters
By adding 40 satellites, Amazon Leo shortens the timeline for achieving the critical mass required for low‑latency broadband service, directly challenging incumbents like SpaceX’s Starlink. Faster deployment also means earlier revenue streams and a stronger bargaining position in the emerging satellite‑Internet market.

The launch underscores Amazon’s commitment to building in‑house aerospace capabilities, creating new skilled jobs and reducing reliance on external launch providers. However, the rapid increase in LEO traffic raises concerns about orbital congestion and the need for robust debris‑mitigation practices.
