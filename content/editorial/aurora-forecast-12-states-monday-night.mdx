A geomagnetic storm expected Monday night could paint the sky with vivid auroras across a swath of the United States. Space weather models from NOAA and the Space Weather Prediction Center indicate that the disturbance will be strong enough for the auroral oval to dip to latitudes as far south as the Ohio Valley. Forecasts highlight twelve states—from Texas up through the Midwest to the Great Lakes—where observers may catch the lights. Such a display offers both a visual treat for the public and a reminder of the Sun’s influence on Earth’s magnetic environment.

## What happened
On November 16, 2026, NOAA’s Space Weather Prediction Center issued a G3 (strong) geomagnetic storm alert, citing a coronal mass ejection that struck Earth’s magnetosphere early Monday.

The storm’s Kp index is projected to reach 6 to 7, a level that typically pushes the auroral oval into mid‑latitude regions. Based on this, the SWPC’s Aurora Forecast map marks potential visibility in Texas, Oklahoma, Arkansas, Missouri, Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, Michigan, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Iowa, and Kentucky.

## Why it matters
A storm of this magnitude can interfere with high‑frequency radio, GPS accuracy, and power‑grid stability, prompting utilities and airlines to take precautionary measures. At the same time, a visible aurora engages the public with space science, potentially inspiring interest in atmospheric and solar research. Local economies in dark‑sky regions may see a short‑term boost as enthusiasts travel to capture the phenomenon.

## How to think about it
Check the latest Kp forecast on the NOAA SWPC website or a reputable aurora‑app before heading out. Aim for locations with minimal artificial lighting—state parks, rural county roads, or designated dark‑sky preserves. Dress warmly, bring a red‑light flashlight to preserve night vision, and keep an eye on local weather for cloud cover. If you’re driving, stay focused on the road; use a passenger to watch the sky while you drive.

<Callout type="warning">Aurora forecasts can shift rapidly; verify conditions on the day of the event and avoid distractions while driving.</Callout>
