The Washington Commanders’ “Command Center” recently launched a playful stock‑exchange style take on three of its young talents, asking fans which player is poised to ‘go to the moon.’ The piece spotlights linebacker Nate Smoot, defensive back Jordan Moss, and quarterback Jayden Daniels (referred to as Paulsen in the release) as potential high‑growth assets. Each player received a vivid analogy—from Smoot’s “Coca‑Cola in 1952” dividend promise to Moss’s rapid learning curve and Daniels’ feedback‑driven improvement. Understanding which of these athletes could deliver the biggest upside matters for fans, fantasy managers, and the franchise’s long‑term planning.
<Callout type="takeaway">Jayden Daniels appears the most likely candidate to ‘go to the moon’ based on his rapid development and feedback loop.</Callout>
## What happened
The article describes Smoot as a first‑round, top‑10 pick who already believes he “plays like a top 10 pick” and likens his future value to “getting in on Coca‑Cola in 1952,” suggesting early dividends with greater returns ahead.
Moss is praised for exceeding expectations given his draft round, with the narrative emphasizing his readiness to contribute now and add value to the “portfolio” of the team.
The piece highlights Daniels (named Paulsen) as a young player who takes feedback quickly, improves from game to game, and is projected to become a top‑five player at his position by season’s end.
## Why it matters
If any of these players deliver the projected upside, the Commanders could see a boost in on‑field performance, fan engagement, and even fantasy football relevance. A breakout season for a high‑draft pick like Smoot validates scouting investments, while an unexpected surge from a later‑round player such as Moss underscores the importance of development pipelines. Daniels’ rapid learning curve could reshape the quarterback hierarchy and influence the team’s strategic direction.

## How to think about it
Evaluate each player through three lenses: (1) current snap count and role in the defensive or offensive scheme, (2) demonstrated ability to translate coaching feedback into on‑field improvement, and (3) the surrounding talent that could either accelerate or hinder their development. Prioritize players who show measurable progress week over week and whose skill set fits the team’s evolving game plan.
