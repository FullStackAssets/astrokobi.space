Elon Musk and former President Donald Trump, once allies during the 2024 campaign, entered a public feud that spilled across X and Truth Social in mid‑2025. The dispute began after Musk criticized the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, a piece of legislation Trump championed, and escalated with personal attacks and claims of ingratitude. Their brief reconciliation at Charlie Kirk’s memorial in September 2025 was quickly undone, leaving a volatile relationship that now shapes public discourse about tech moguls in politics. Understanding this clash is essential for anyone tracking how billionaire tech influence can sway democratic processes and future policy.

<Callout type="takeaway">Musk’s feud with Trump illustrates how personal rivalries among tech leaders can reshape political power dynamics.</Callout>

## What happened

From June to September 2025, Musk and Trump exchanged a series of hostile posts on their respective platforms, X and Truth Social, after Musk publicly denounced the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. During this period, Musk claimed responsibility for Trump’s 2024 electoral victory and later accused Trump of appearing in the Epstein files, intensifying the personal nature of the dispute. The two briefly appeared together at Charlie Kirk’s memorial service in September 2025, suggesting a temporary thaw, but the underlying tensions resurfaced shortly thereafter.

The feud revived earlier animosities that date back to Musk’s criticism of Trump before the 2016 election and a series of exchanges in 2022. By mid‑2025, Trump labeled Musk a “bullshit artist” and warned that Musk could owe billions for alleged financial missteps, while Musk dismissed Trump as too old for leadership and hinted at supporting other Republican candidates.

## Why it matters

The public nature of the Musk‑Trump clash highlights the growing entanglement of technology platforms with electoral politics. When the owners of major social media channels engage in personal disputes, the resulting content can amplify misinformation, polarize audiences, and affect voter perceptions. Moreover, the feud underscores the strategic leverage tech CEOs possess: their statements can legitimize or delegitimize political figures, influencing policy debates such as the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Stakeholders ranging from regulators to investors must monitor how these power struggles could reshape governance of digital public squares.

## How to think about it

Treat the Musk‑Trump episode as a signal rather than a definitive trend. Track whether similar disputes emerge among other tech leaders and political figures, and assess the impact on platform policy changes. When evaluating news from X or Truth Social, cross‑verify with independent outlets to separate personal drama from policy relevance. For policymakers, consider frameworks that limit direct political campaigning by platform owners while preserving free expression.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that every online spat between tech CEOs and politicians will translate into concrete legislative outcomes.</Callout>
