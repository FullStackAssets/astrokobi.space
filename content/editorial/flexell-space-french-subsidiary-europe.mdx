Flexell Space, a Korean developer of tandem solar cells for spacecraft, announced a new French entity on Tuesday. The subsidiary, FLEXELL SPACE FRANCE SAS, will be based in Paris and serve as a local hub for European customers. By situating a team on the continent, the company hopes to speed technical reviews, supply planning and contract negotiations. The move comes as the firm deepens talks with multiple European satellite operators about volume production of its next‑generation solar panels. If successful, the presence could reshape how space‑power hardware is sourced across Europe.

<Callout type="takeaway">A Paris‑based office lets Flexell Space turn European interest into concrete solar‑cell contracts faster.</Callout>

## What happened
Flexell Space created FLEXELL SPACE FRANCE SAS in Paris to provide on‑the‑ground support for ongoing contract and volume‑production discussions with European customers. The new entity will handle everything from technical documentation reviews and sample evaluations to environmental test coordination and supply‑term negotiations.

The company said its European talks now span France, Germany, Italy and Lithuania, with each customer requiring detailed alignment on performance, dimensions, reliability and delivery schedules. By having a local point of contact, Flexell aims to shorten response times and better align its Korean R&D and manufacturing teams with European design cycles.

Flexell’s core technology is a tandem architecture that layers solar cells of differing bandgaps to boost efficiency in the harsh space environment. The French office will act as a bridge to bring this next‑generation technology from prototype to production‑ready status for European spacecraft.

## Why it matters
A local subsidiary reduces the friction that typically slows cross‑continental aerospace collaborations, allowing faster iteration on design and testing. European satellite operators gain a more responsive partner, which can accelerate schedule certainty for missions that depend on high‑efficiency power. The move also signals growing confidence in the commercial viability of advanced space solar cells, potentially prompting other non‑European firms to establish a foothold in the EU market.
