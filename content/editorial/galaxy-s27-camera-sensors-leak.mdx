The tech community got a rare look at Samsung’s upcoming Galaxy S27 lineup, with detailed specs of each model’s camera hardware. A leak from SammyGuru lists the exact sensors being tested for the S27, S27+, S27 Pro, and S27 Ultra. The data shows a 200‑megapixel main sensor on the higher‑end phones while the lower models stick with the familiar 50‑megapixel unit. Because camera performance drives flagship sales, these revelations shape consumer expectations and Samsung’s competitive stance.

<Callout type="takeaway">Samsung’s S27 series upgrades the main camera on premium models but leaves most other lenses largely unchanged.</Callout>

## What happened
Both the S27 and S27+ retain the same camera stack used in the previous generation: a 50 MP main sensor that could be either Sony’s IMX955 or Samsung’s S5KGNG, a 12 MP ultrawide lens powered by Sony’s IMX564 (in service since the S23), a 3× telephoto module using Samsung’s S5K3K1, and a front‑facing selfie sensor that may be Samsung’s S5K3LU or Sony’s IMX874.

The higher‑end S27 Pro and S27 Ultra share a new 200 MP primary sensor, identified as Samsung’s S5KHP6. Apart from the main shooter, the rest of the lens array mirrors the lower‑tier devices, meaning the ultrawide and telephoto modules remain unchanged. The leak also notes that Samsung is testing both its own ISOCELL line and Sony sensors, suggesting a possible shift away from exclusive in‑house parts.

These details line up with earlier rumors that Samsung would not dramatically overhaul its camera suite, instead focusing the biggest sensor jump on the flagship tier while keeping cost‑effective components for the more affordable models.

## Why it matters
Camera hardware is a primary differentiator in the premium smartphone market; a 200 MP sensor promises higher resolution and better low‑light performance, potentially narrowing the gap with competing flagships. However, retaining older ultrawide and telephoto modules may limit overall image‑quality gains, tempering excitement for consumers who expected a full‑scale refresh. Samsung’s mixed‑sensor strategy also signals how the company balances innovation costs against the need to stay price‑competitive across its lineup.

## How to think about it
Treat the S27 series as an incremental evolution rather than a generational overhaul. If you prioritize raw resolution for landscape or studio work, the Ultra’s 200 MP sensor is a clear advantage. For everyday photography, the unchanged ultrawide and telephoto lenses mean performance will be similar to the S26 generation, so weigh the price premium against the actual benefit you’ll see. Consider waiting for software updates that often unlock the full potential of new sensors before making a purchase.

<Callout type="warning">Don’t assume the 200 MP sensor automatically translates to better real‑world photos; sensor size, lens quality, and software processing all play crucial roles.</Callout>
