Greta Thunberg took the stage on the fourth day of COP31 in Antalya, delivering a stark warning that the planet is "being killed" by the very actors expected to rescue it. The 23‑year‑old activist linked climate breakdown to broader crises, including war and human‑rights violations, and called for immediate, substantive action. Her remarks arrived as world leaders, including the sole G7 prime minister present, offered tepid pledges amid growing frustration over stalled progress. The speech reignited debate over the effectiveness of high‑profile activism in shaping global climate policy.

<Callout type="takeaway">Greta Thunberg’s COP31 address frames climate inaction as a direct, lethal threat demanding urgent, concrete response.</Callout>

## What happened
Thunberg told the assembled delegates that "the planet is not dying, it is being killed and we know by who," accusing governments of complicit destruction. She emphasized that no rescue is forthcoming and urged activists to become "the adult in the room" defending human rights. The speech also connected climate failure to rising conflicts, warning of a future marked by "more genocides and more ecocides."

The remarks coincided with British Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s speech, in which he labeled the UK's recent heatwave "not normal" and pledged to push the nation toward net‑zero emissions. While Burnham’s appearance was praised as a positive signal, climate‑justice adviser Beth John cautioned that presence alone is insufficient without substantive delivery.

## Why it matters
Thunberg’s framing shifts climate discourse from abstract future risk to present‑day mortality, potentially reshaping public perception and political urgency. By linking environmental collapse to human‑rights abuses and conflict, she broadens the coalition of stakeholders who may feel compelled to act. The speech also pressures governments to move beyond symbolic gestures toward measurable emissions reductions, influencing upcoming negotiations and national policy roadmaps.

## How to think about it
Treat Thunberg’s address as a diagnostic tool rather than a policy blueprint. Assess where her warnings intersect with concrete legislative proposals in your region, and prioritize actions that deliver measurable emissions cuts. Use the speech to rally cross‑sector partnerships—environmental NGOs, human‑rights groups, and peace advocates—to build a unified lobbying front. Finally, track subsequent national commitments for alignment with the urgency she articulated.
