## What happened
JWST’s deep‑field observations revealed a population of bright, red galaxies—dubbed Little Red Dots (LRDs)—that are far more common than cosmological models predict.

Early explanations ranged from nascent quasars to dusty galaxies, but none could simultaneously account for the abundance and spectral features observed.

Recent simulations by Dr. Daniel Whalen’s team suggest that LRDs are the remnants of supermassive primordial stars that collapsed within the first few hundred million years after the Big Bang, reproducing the observed colors and luminosities.

## Why it matters
If LRDs are indeed collapsed primordial stars, this would provide a natural route to the rapid growth of supermassive black holes in the early universe, challenging the prevailing direct‑collapse black‑hole scenario.

It would also reshape models of early galaxy formation and influence predictions for the epoch of reionization, affecting future surveys by JWST, ALMA, and next‑generation telescopes.

## How to think about it
Approach the theory by examining the simulation assumptions—such as gas cooling rates, metallicity, and radiation feedback—and comparing the predicted spectra with independent observations.

Consider alternative explanations and look for signatures that uniquely distinguish collapsed‑star remnants from direct‑collapse black holes.

Maintain flexibility: as new data arrive, be ready to refine or replace the model.
