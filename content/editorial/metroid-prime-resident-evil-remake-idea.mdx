Metroid Prime 4: Beyond arrived on the Nintendo Switch in 2025 after five years of development, but its lukewarm reception left long‑time fans uneasy. Critics pointed to filler side characters, an underused bike mechanic, and an ending that felt unsatisfying. The disappointment is striking because the series has been a benchmark for sci‑fi shooters since its 2002 debut on the GameCube. With the original trilogy still missing official Switch releases, the franchise’s momentum feels stalled. A fresh approach could restore the series’ reputation.

<Callout type="takeaway">A Resident Evil‑style trilogy remake could give Metroid Prime the modern polish it needs to regain its shooter mojo.</Callout>

## What happened
Metroid Prime 4: Beyond debuted on the Switch in 2025 after a protracted five‑year development cycle at Retro Studios. While the game finally delivered the series’ signature first‑person exploration, reviewers highlighted annoying side characters, a bike that felt tacked on, and an ending that failed to deliver the narrative payoff fans expected after an 18‑year gap since Prime 3.

Capcom’s recent success with Resident Evil remakes—starting with the 2002 overhaul and continuing through the acclaimed 2019 and 2023 releases—demonstrates how classic titles can be reimagined with modern graphics, tighter controls, and heightened tension. The article proposes applying that same “recapturing the vibes” mindset to the Metroid Prime trilogy, using the Prime 4 engine as a foundation for a unified, contemporary experience.

## Why it matters
Reviving the original Metroid Prime games would address a glaring gap in Nintendo’s Switch library, where flagship titles like The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time have already been refreshed for modern hardware. A high‑quality remake could re‑engage lapsed fans, attract new players, and reaffirm Nintendo’s commitment to its sci‑fi shooter heritage. Moreover, a successful remake could set a precedent for other dormant franchises, showing that strategic nostalgia can coexist with innovation.

## How to think about it
Treat the remake as a platform upgrade rather than a substitute for new content. Evaluate the project on three axes: technical fidelity (graphics, performance), gameplay refinement (controls, pacing), and brand impact (fan sentiment, market reach). If the remake scores high on fidelity and refinement while delivering measurable buzz, it can serve as a launchpad for subsequent original titles that build on the refreshed engine.

<Callout type="warning">Avoid launching the remake without clear communication about its scope; overpromising features that aren’t delivered can damage the franchise’s credibility.</Callout>
