On Nov 13, 2026 SpaceX will attempt the 15th flight test of its Starship megarocket from Starbase in Texas. The 75‑minute launch window opens at 5:45 p.m. EST, just before sunset, giving a dramatic twilight launch. After booster separation, the 171‑foot upper stage (Ship) will orbit Earth about 14 times over 22 hours, deploying 26 Starlink V3 satellites. Because the vehicle is huge and reflects sunlight, skywatchers along the Gulf Coast may see it as a bright moving point in the twilight sky. Seeing the launch offers a rare chance to connect everyday observers with a historic reusable launch system.

## What happened
SpaceX’s Starship is slated for a 75‑minute launch window that begins at 5:45 p.m. EST on Nov 13, launching from the Starbase facility in South Texas. The Super Heavy booster will accelerate the Ship upper stage through the densest part of the atmosphere before returning for a powered landing, while Ship will complete roughly 14 orbits in the next 22 hours and release a payload of 26 Starlink V3 satellites.

The upper stage measures about 171 feet (52 meters) tall, making it one of the largest objects visible from the ground. Experts say it will appear considerably brighter than the regular Starlink constellation, and observers can expect to see it for about 90 minutes after sunset on Nov 13 and again before sunrise on Nov 14 as it catches sunlight against Earth’s shadow.

## Why it matters
Public visibility turns a high‑tech launch into a shared cultural event, sparking interest in space exploration and demonstrating the practicality of fully reusable rockets. At the same time, a bright, low‑orbiting object adds to the growing number of artificial lights in the night sky, raising questions about light pollution and satellite tracking for astronomers.

## How to think about it
Start by checking your local sunset time for Nov 13 and plan to look eastward shortly after dark. Use a star‑mapping app to locate the horizon line and track the expected path of the Ship’s orbit. No telescopes are required; the vehicle’s size and reflectivity make it visible to the naked eye, though binoculars can help confirm its motion. Keep an eye on official SpaceX updates in case the launch is delayed, and remember that visibility will improve as the vehicle moves away from the sunlit horizon.

<Callout type="warning">Never look directly at the rocket or its plume while the sun is above the horizon; use proper eye protection or wait until it is in shadow.</Callout>
