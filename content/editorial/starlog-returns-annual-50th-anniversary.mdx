After a 17‑year hiatus, the iconic sci‑fi magazine Starlog is back as an annual print edition, timed for its 50th anniversary. The first issue, #375, hit pre‑order on Nov. 13, 2026, reviving a publication that first launched in 1976. Starlog’s return reflects a growing fatigue with purely digital media and a nostalgic demand for tactile, curated content. For longtime fans, the revival offers a bridge between classic genre coverage and today’s evolving sci‑fi landscape.
<Callout type="takeaway">Starlog’s comeback shows a renewed appetite for tactile sci‑fi storytelling amid digital fatigue.</Callout>
## What happened
Starlog debuted in 1976, pre‑Star Wars, with a painted Star Trek cover and quickly became the go‑to source for genre news, interviews, and VFX features. It ran for 33 years before folding in 2009 amid declining subscriptions, leaving a 17‑year gap in print.
On Nov. 13, 2026, Fangoria Holdings and io9 co‑founder Annalee Newitz announced the magazine’s return as a once‑a‑year print issue. The anniversary edition showcases cover art by Armenian illustrator Gabriel Yeganyan and promises the same mix of deep‑dive interviews, retrospectives, and con coverage that defined the original run.
## Why it matters
The revival signals that niche print media can still find an audience when digital saturation creates fatigue. By offering a curated, tactile experience, Starlog reconnects fans with the communal rituals of flipping pages and collecting issues, reinforcing cultural memory around franchises like Star Wars, Star Trek, and Alien. Its success could encourage other legacy titles to explore limited‑run print models, reshaping how genre journalism balances online immediacy with physical permanence.

## How to think about it
Treat the new Starlog as a collectible rather than a primary news source; enjoy its deep‑dive features while supplementing daily updates with online platforms. If you pre‑order, consider the limited print run and potential shipping delays, and weigh the value of owning a tangible piece of sci‑fi heritage against the cost. Supporting the edition also helps gauge demand for future print ventures in the genre.
<Callout type="warning">Pre‑order now but be aware that the first issue is a limited‑edition run; supply may run out before all interested readers receive a copy.</Callout>
