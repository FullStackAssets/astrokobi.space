Golden State Warriors guard Stephen Curry suffered an oblique strain that was confirmed as a tear during Thursday’s practice. Team doctors announced he will be sidelined for a minimum of two weeks, ruling him out of the upcoming game against the Los Angeles Lakers. The injury comes as the Warriors chase a top seed in the Western Conference, and Curry’s absence reshapes their offensive strategy.

Curry, a two‑time MVP and the league’s leading scorer, has missed only a handful of games in his career. The oblique tear is the first significant soft‑tissue injury he has faced since his 2022 ankle sprain. The team expects to reassess his workload before clearing him for a return.

<Callout type="takeaway">Curry’s two‑week absence forces the Warriors to rely on depth and could alter the West’s playoff picture.</Callout>

## What happened

The Warriors reported that Curry experienced a sudden pull in his right oblique during a routine shooting drill. An MRI confirmed a grade‑II tear, prompting the medical staff to prescribe rest and a gradual rehabilitation protocol. The official statement set a minimum two‑week recovery window, though the exact return date will depend on pain levels and functional testing.

## Why it matters

Curry accounts for roughly 30% of Golden State’s scoring output and is the primary facilitator in their motion offense. Without him, the team must lean on Klay Thompson, Andrew Wiggins, and emerging guard Jordan Poole to generate offense, which could reduce efficiency against elite defenses. In the tightly contested Western Conference, missing Curry for even a short stretch may shift seeding battles and affect betting markets.
