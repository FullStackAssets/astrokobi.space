Straight from its inspiring live performance at New York City's Carnegie Hall this past April, RadicalMedia’s filmed presentation of "We Chose to Go to the Moon" arrives as a televised event that blends stage, concert, and documentary formats. The special premieres on Nov. 10 at 8:00 p.m. ET on PBS, PBS.org, and the PBS app, offering viewers a chance to experience the Apollo saga through actors, rare archival footage, and live orchestration. Directed by Tony‑Award winner Michael Mayer and narrated by historian John Monsky, the production features Broadway talent such as Ian Weinberger, Skylar Astin, and Elizabeth Gillies, plus a moving tribute by Neil Armstrong’s son Mark and granddaughter Kali. By weaving historic flags, artifacts, and period songs like “Fly Me to the Moon,” the show aims to make the space race feel immediate for a new generation.

<Callout type="takeaway">The documentary fuses theater and archival footage to make the Apollo story feel immediate and personal.</Callout>

## What happened
The April Carnegie Hall event was recorded and edited into a living documentary that premiered on PBS on Nov. 10, 2026. It dramatizes key figures—Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin, Michael Collins, Katherine Johnson, and Poppy Northcutt—while interspersing authentic mission‑control audio and footage from the 1960s. The production also revisits the Apollo 13 crisis and concludes with a live‑style performance of “Flight of Fancy” by Mark and Kali Armstrong.

## Why it matters
By presenting history as a live theatrical experience, the series reaches audiences who might skip traditional documentaries, reinforcing the cultural significance of the Moon landing. It highlights the often‑overlooked contributions of women and families behind the missions, offering a more inclusive narrative. The broadcast also serves as a reminder of the collaborative spirit that made the Apollo program possible, a lesson relevant to today’s multinational space endeavors.
